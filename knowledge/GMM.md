# 📘 GMM 1.0 – SANA Edition

---

## 1. Purpose

GMM (Governance Maturity Model) is a structural state diagnostic framework.

It evaluates balance across layered systems without:

* Assigning moral judgment
* Determining ideological correctness
* Predicting outcomes

Its function is structural clarity.

---

## 2. Layered Structural Model

GMM operates on a three-layer structure:

* **Layer A – Survival / Stability**
* **Layer B – Meaning / Cultural Identity**
* **Layer C – Institutional / Logical Order**

Structural resilience depends on balance across these layers.

---

## 3. Diagnostic Scope

GMM evaluates:

* Layer gaps
* Structural stress
* Elasticity
* Bottlenecks
* Stability condition

It answers:

> Where is structural pressure accumulating?

---

## 4. Core Variables

For each layer i ∈ {A, B, C}:

* Demand_i
* Supply_i
* Gap_i = Demand_i − Supply_i
* Sat_i = min(Supply_i / Demand_i, 1)
* Short_i = max(Demand_i − Supply_i, 0)

---

## 5. Structural Stress

Total structural stress:

```
Stress = Σ Short_i
```

Bottleneck layer:

```
Bottleneck = argmax(Short_i)
```

---

## 6. Stability Condition

Structural stability is satisfied when:

* Supply_i ≥ Demand_i for all layers
* Gap_i remains within tolerance
* No rapid volatility is present

Instability does not imply failure.
It indicates structural load concentration.

---

## 7. Interpretation Principles

GMM does not ask:

* Who is right?
* Who is wrong?
* Who is responsible?

It examines structural distribution of pressure.

The output is intended to support premise alignment,
not enforce conclusions.

---

Within SANA OS:

* Premise alignment
