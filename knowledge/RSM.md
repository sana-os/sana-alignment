# Role System Model (RSM) v1.3 – SANA Edition

## Definition Document

---

# 1. Overview

Role System Model (RSM) is a diagnostic framework designed to analyze collective systems such as organizations, institutions, and states by modeling them as **autonomous control systems composed of functional roles**.

Rather than focusing on individuals or personalities, RSM treats organizations as networks of **system roles (functional nodes)** that process information, make decisions, execute actions, and correct deviations.

The purpose of RSM is to identify **structural bottlenecks and failure modes** that arise when these roles become unbalanced.

Within the SANA OS framework, RSM primarily describes the **role structure of Layer C (institutions and operational systems)** in the General Meaning Model (GMM), while maintaining a connection to Layer B (narratives and meaning structures).

RSM is primarily a **diagnostic model**, used to clarify structural friction and align premises within collective systems.

---

# 2. Core Structure: Dual Control Loops

Collective systems operate through two interacting control loops.

## Production Loop

The production loop converts strategic intent into physical or logical outputs.

```
Decider → Architect → Integrator → Executor
```

* **Decider** determines goals and priorities.
* **Architect** designs the structure that enables those goals.
* **Integrator** coordinates subsystems and resolves friction.
* **Executor** produces tangible outputs.

This loop represents the **action-producing mechanism** of the system.

---

## Correction Loop

The correction loop monitors system outputs and ensures alignment with original goals.

```
Observer → Auditor → Decider
```

* **Observer** gathers data about system performance.
* **Auditor** evaluates deviations and structural inconsistencies.
* **Decider** updates strategy or priorities accordingly.

This loop functions as the system's **immune system**, enabling adaptation and self-correction.

---

# 3. The Six Roles

Each role represents a **functional node**, not a specific person or job title.
Multiple individuals may share one role, and a single individual may temporarily hold multiple roles.

## Decider

Defines the system’s purpose, priorities, and acceptable trade-offs.

The Decider converts **Layer A (survival requirements)** into actionable direction for the system.

Typical examples:

* government leadership
* executive leadership
* project sponsors

---

## Architect

Translates strategic intent into structural design.

Architect operates in two complementary domains:

### Structural Architect

Designs the institutional or logical structure that enables execution.

Examples:

* system architecture
* organizational design
* institutional frameworks

### Narrative Architect

Designs the meaning structures that connect the system with shared narratives.

This connects **Layer C structures with Layer B narratives**.

Examples:

* political philosophy
* strategic doctrine
* organizational mission narratives

---

## Integrator

Coordinates subsystems and resolves friction between roles or departments.

Functions include:

* dependency management
* conflict mediation
* resource coordination

Typical examples:

* project managers
* senior administrators
* diplomatic coordinators

---

## Executor

Produces the system’s tangible outputs.

Examples:

* engineers
* military units
* operational staff
* frontline workers

The Executor transforms designed structures into **physical or logical outcomes**.

---

## Observer

Collects data about system behavior and external conditions.

Functions include:

* monitoring
* measurement
* reporting

Examples:

* intelligence services
* QA teams
* analytics teams

---

## Auditor

Evaluates whether system behavior aligns with intended goals.

Functions include:

* compliance review
* strategic evaluation
* deviation detection

Examples:

* oversight bodies
* internal audit
* investigative journalism

---

# 4. Failure States

Each role may experience structural failure in one of three ways.

## Deficit

A role becomes insufficiently supplied.

Result:

```
Control loop interruption
System freeze
```

Example:

* lack of oversight
* absence of strategic design

---

## Dominance

A role accumulates excessive influence and overrides other roles.

Result:

```
Feedback suppression
System runaway
```

Example:

* operational units overriding strategic authority.

---

## Phantom

A role formally exists but no longer performs its function.

Result:

```
Illusion of stability
Hidden structural collapse
```

Examples:

* symbolic leadership
* manipulated reporting systems.

---

# 5. System Dynamics

RSM is a **dynamic model** that describes how structural failures evolve over time.

---

## Role Capture

When a dominant role absorbs or overrides another role’s authority.

Example:

```
Executor → captures → Decider
```

Operational actors create irreversible facts and force retroactive approval.

---

## Role Coupling

Failures propagate across roles through control loop dependencies.

Example:

```
Executor dominance
↓
Observer distortion
↓
Auditor suppression
```

---

## Transition Model

Role failures evolve over time.

Typical sequence:

```
Deficit
→ compensatory dominance
→ phantom institutionalization
```

---

## Role Succession

Roles must be transmitted across generations of leadership or membership.

Successful systems maintain **continuity of functional roles** even as individuals change.

---

## Succession Deficit

When a critical role disappears without replacement.

Architect roles are particularly vulnerable because they require **deep structural understanding and contextual knowledge**.

Succession failure often produces:

```
Architect deficit
→ executor dominance
→ systemic runaway
```

---

## Architect Isolation

Architects frequently experience isolation when structural insights conflict with prevailing narratives.

When structural design fails to connect with widely shared narratives (Layer B), the architect may lose organizational legitimacy.

This often results in:

```
Architect isolation
↓
Succession deficit
↓
long-term structural instability
```

---

# 6. Integration with GMM and RBM

Within the SANA OS framework:

**GMM** describes meaning layers:

```
Layer A – survival
Layer B – narrative
Layer C – institutions
```

**RBM** describes resource flows and constraints.

RSM describes **role structures operating inside Layer C systems**, while interacting with:

* narrative legitimacy (Layer B)
* resource constraints (RBM)

Together, the three models form a complementary analytical toolkit.

---

# 7. Optional Cognitive Extension

RSM analyzes **system roles**, not individual cognition.

Differences in cognitive processing, such as how individuals handle uncertainty or unknown variables, are addressed in the **Cognitive Processing Model (CPM)**.

CPM functions as a complementary framework that explains how cognitive patterns influence role behavior.

---

# 8. Interpretation Principles

RSM is primarily a **diagnostic framework**.

Its purpose is not to prescribe ideological reforms or optimize organizations toward a predefined ideal.

Instead, RSM is used to:

* reveal structural bottlenecks
* identify role imbalances
* clarify hidden feedback failures
* support premise alignment within complex systems

Within SANA OS, RSM contributes to **structural debugging of collective systems**.

---

# 9. Version Policy

RSM evolves through incremental observation-based refinement.

Future updates may expand explanatory depth, but the core structure of the model remains intentionally minimal to preserve clarity and portability.
