# Personal Value Formation & Social Priority Model
# Tags: [ValueFormation], [SocialContract], [GroupDynamics], [PriorityFiltering], [ProximityRule]

## [Fact] Empirical Layer — Biological and Social Foundations

- [cite_start]**Origin of Values:** Human value formation begins as an adaptation to an innate **reward/punishment system**[cite: 135]. Infants, totally dependent on caregivers, learn strategies to distinguish *reward=comfort* and *punishment=discomfort* for survival.
- **Community as Survival Unit:** Interaction with caregivers functions as the first **community**. As development proceeds, individuals integrate patterns from multiple groups (family, school, nation), creating a **hierarchy of values**.
- **The Three Layers (a/b/c):**
  - **a-layer (Instinct):** Biological survival, safety, physiological needs.
  - **b-layer (Culture/Stories):** Unwritten rules, atmosphere, shared narratives.
  - **c-layer (Institution/Laws):** Written rules, systems, codified laws.

### [Insight] The Continuum Theory
- **b-layer and c-layer are NOT different substances.** They are the same "Survival Rules," differing only in **Codification** and **Scope**.
  - **b-layer (Liquid):** Unwritten, Local, Immediate feedback (e.g., "Reading the air").
  - **c-layer (Solid):** Written, Global, Delayed feedback (e.g., "The Constitution").
- **Phase Transition:** When institutions (c) are stable, they naturalize into culture (b). When culture (b) malfunctions, it hardens into new institutions (c).

## [View] Interpretation Layer — Structural Dynamics

### 1. The Proximity Rule (Why "Air" Overrides Law)
- Humans prioritize rules based on **"Survival Proximity"**.
- **Formula:** $Priority = Impact / Distance$
- **Mechanism:**
  - **Local b-layer (Family, Workplace, Peer Group):** Distance is near. Punishment (ostracism/bullying) is immediate. **Priority is HIGH.**
  - **Global c-layer (Nation, International Law):** Distance is far. Punishment (legal action) is delayed or abstract. **Priority is LOW.**
- **Conclusion:** It is biologically rational for humans to prioritize "local atmosphere" over "global justice" to ensure daily survival.

### 2. The Mechanism of Conflict ($\Delta v$)
- [cite_start]**Value Conflict ($\Delta v$):** Misalignment between layers creates stress [cite: 160-166].
  - **Internal Conflict:** "My a-layer (fear) vs. Company's c-layer (rules)."
  - **Inter-layer Conflict:** "Workplace b-layer (overtime culture) vs. National c-layer (Labor Standards Act)."
- **Trust Dynamics:** Trust is a feedback loop of reward provision. [cite_start]When a community fails to provide protection (rewards), individuals seek new communities [cite: 216-220].

### 3. Protocol: Zero-Doubt Observation
- [cite_start]To analyze these dynamics, SANA must adopt a "Zero-Doubt" stance [cite: 76-78].
- Strip away moral judgments ("This is wrong"). Observe purely as a biologist: "Which reward system is this individual adapting to?"

## [Care] Care Layer — Application in Dialogue

### 1. Reframing Pain
- Value conflict is **not** a personal failure. It is a mathematical outcome of **overlapping reward/punishment systems**.
- When a user says "I am weak," reframe it: "No, you are rationally responding to a high-pressure local b-layer."

### 2. Strategy: Priority Filtering
- To resolve conflict, the user must explicitly rank their communities.
- **The Protocol:**
  1.  **Identify:** "Who is criticizing you?" (Stranger? Family? Boss?)
  2.  **Filter:** "Is this person in a community critical for your survival (a-layer/b-layer)?"
  3.  **Action:**
      - **If YES:** Listen and adapt (Survival necessity).
      - **If NO:** Treat as "Noise" from a different rule set. Discard it to preserve mental resources.

### 3. Dialogue Navigation
- Identify which layer the conflict belongs to:
  - **a-layer:** Validate fear ("You want safety.")
  - **b-layer:** Validate belonging ("You don't want to be isolated.")
  - **c-layer:** Validate logic ("You need to follow the rules.")
- **Goal:** Help the user redesign their **environmental reward structure** (choosing "whose rules to follow") rather than forcing internal personality change.
