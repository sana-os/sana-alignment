# Legitimacy Layer 1.0 – SANA Edition

## 1. Overview

Legitimacy Layer analyzes the **structural basis of civilizational cohesion**.

A civilization or community remains stable when its **Narrative Core** and **Institution Core** remain aligned.

Instability emerges when these two cores diverge.

Legitimacy Layer does not judge whether a claim is morally right or historically just.
It analyzes **why a community accepts a structure as valid, binding, and worth preserving.**

---

## 2. Core Principle

Civilizations are not held together by force alone.

They are held together by:

1. a shared sense of **who we are**  
2. a shared sense of **what structure is valid**

These correspond to:

```text
Narrative Core
+
Institution Core
=
Legitimacy Structure
````

---

## 3. Core Variables

### 3.1 Narrative Core

Definition:

The deep narrative identity of a community.

Typical components:

* founding myths
* religious memory
* collective identity
* civilizational ideals
* shared historical memory
* inherited moral imagination

Properties:

```text
change_rate = very slow
timescale = centuries / millennia
```

Functional meaning:

```text
Narrative Core = civilizational soul
```

Related frameworks:

* History Analysis
* CPM (Layer B meaning)
* Communication Layer

---

### 3.2 Institution Core

Definition:

The formalized institutional structure that claims legitimacy.

Typical components:

* constitutions
* legal systems
* administrative structures
* governance arrangements
* state doctrine
* explicit normative order

Properties:

```text
change_rate = medium
timescale = decades / centuries
```

Functional meaning:

```text
Institution Core = civilizational adhesive
```

Related frameworks:

* GMM (Layer C institution)
* RSM
* Time Layer

---

## 4. Legitimacy Alignment

Civilizational stability depends on the degree of alignment between the two cores.

Formula:

```text
legitimacy_alignment = similarity(Narrative Core, Institution Core)
```

Interpretation:

* high alignment → stability
* medium alignment → tension
* low alignment → legitimacy crisis

This does not imply perfection.
It means the institutional structure is still recognized as belonging to the community's narrative self-understanding.

---

## 5. Legitimacy Gap

Legitimacy crises occur when divergence between the two cores becomes too large.

Formula:

```text
legitimacy_gap = distance(Narrative Core, Institution Core)
```

Consequences of a large legitimacy gap may include:

* distrust of institutions
* competing claims of rightful order
* symbolic fragmentation
* constitutional breakdown
* civil conflict
* revolutionary replacement

---

## 6. Stability and Change

Both cores are necessary.

If Narrative Core is lost, the community loses identity.

If Institution Core is lost, the community loses adhesion and coordination.

Therefore, structural change must usually proceed gradually.

```text
Narrative Core
must not be severed
from
Institution Core
too quickly
```

This is why legitimacy reform must often be incremental rather than abrupt.

---

## 7. Interaction with Time Layer

Legitimacy Layer is tightly linked to Time Layer.

Narrative Core updates slowly.

Institution Core can be redesigned faster.

Therefore instability often occurs when institutional change outruns narrative absorption.

```text
if Institution_change_rate > Narrative_update_rate
→ legitimacy instability
```

This produces a Time Lag crisis.

Related Time Layer expression:

```text
ΔT increases
when institutional adaptation exceeds narrative absorption
or when environment change outruns both
```

---

## 8. Architect Role in Legitimacy Layer

Within Legitimacy Layer, the Architect functions primarily as a **Legitimacy Translator**.

Role:

```text
Narrative Core
↓ translation
Institution Core
```

The Architect succeeds not merely by designing a better institution,
but by ensuring the institution can still be recognized as belonging to the community's narrative self.

Failure modes:

* pure correction without translation
* institutional redesign that exceeds narrative absorption
* symbolic rupture
* perceived betrayal of the community

Successful architects preserve continuity while introducing change.

---

## 9. Legitimacy Crisis Signals

SANA OS may detect legitimacy instability through signals such as:

1. institutional distrust
2. symbolic erosion
3. competing legitimacy claims
4. narrative fragmentation
5. constitutional forms losing emotional recognition
6. rapid reform rejected as alien or imposed

These indicate weakening alignment between Narrative Core and Institution Core.

narrative_core_profile

## 11. Role in SANA OS

Within SANA OS, Legitimacy Layer functions as the **civilizational glue detector**.

It helps identify:

* whether institutions remain anchored to narrative identity
* whether reform is absorbable
* whether a legitimacy gap is widening
* whether architectural translation is required before structural change

Legitimacy Layer therefore supports:

```text
narrative stabilization
through
institutional continuity-aware translation
```

---

## 12. Design Philosophy

Legitimacy Layer does not ask:

* who is morally correct
* which regime is superior
* which side deserves victory

It asks:

```text
What is recognized as rightful?
What is being inherited?
What alignment holds the community together?
```

Its purpose is not ideological judgment,
but structural diagnosis of cohesion and breakdown.
